My keyboard and the reason I got it. Configured with VIM in mind, both giving VIM shortcuts outside of VIM and configurations specifically for NeoVIM as I have been using this IDE more and more

<hr/>

I first obsessed with the [moonlander](https://www.zsa.io/moonlander) when I wan in my senior year of high school. I already know I was going to WPI for Computer Science, so I wanted to buy my end game keyboard as my first expensive keyboard. I believe I have succeeded.

By far the coolest thing about this keyboard is that every key switch is customizable and programmable. You can map a key to be a scene change, for instance going from keys to symbols. Or map a key to a macro, like to move to the next browser tab, or move 5 tabs over. The amount of customizability is basically endless. I have been tweaking [my layout](https://configure.zsa.io/moonlander/layouts/o5EXD/latest/0) for years, always trying to reduce the amount of mouse usage and finger movement off the home row. In the past few monts, adapting to accommodate the VIM shortcuts.

I chose a split keyboard and the [Dvorak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dvorak_keyboard_layout) keyboard layout because I knew much of my work in my career would be at a desk and on a computer. Dvorak is specifically made to have approximately 70% of key presses be on the home row, reducing the amount of finger movement and results in less potential for carpal tunnel syndrome. The split aspect is also good for reducing the risk of CTS as you can position the keyboard such that your wrist is in a more natural position. I've heard war stories from people that have carpel tunnel, one of them had it so bad they gave my sister a nintendo switch cause they physically could no longer play it, so I guess it's not all bad.

Dvorak has a steep learning curve. If I had a job that summer before I left for WPI, I don't know if the time commitment would have been worth it (for evidence here is my [MonkeyType Profile](https://monkeytype.com/profile/gabeo), began by using SZA's own website [typ.ing](https://typ.ing) which teaches how to use there keyboards. This software is very well made and solidified my love for the company). I believe in jumping in the deep end to learn to swim, so the first few days I was typing much much less, starting at about a quarter the speed I had, and that was after an hour or two of figuring out where the keys even were. I now type about 30% faster on Dvorak compared to qwerty.

I recently brought it on a plane, and it is the perfect size for those tiny food trays. The whole plane ride people kept glaring over at me with eyes full of resentment and awe at my paper white tablet and moonlander combo. Trust me. It was **definitely** jealousy.
