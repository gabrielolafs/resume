My apartment in Worcester was not a place of beauty. It was a place to sleep at night, and in the winter months, under 4 blankets. For the sake of my roommates, the networking had to be easily deployed and functional, but I also wanted the ability to mess around a little. After a conversation with a coworker who's job it is to be a good network engineer, I opted for a Ubiquity setup, and I could not be more happy with the decision. Instead of renting a router from our ISP for two years, I chose to spend 20 dollars more up front for an Express 7 router, a Lite 8 PoE switch, and 2 U6+ Access Points. Plus I kept it all after the fact, deploying them in my [home home lab](/articles/home-network) as a dedicated lab network with only my equipment connected to it.

##### Pi-Hole:
This is my local solution for DNS. I use it to access the things like the vnc connection to [PRORM](#prorm-programmer-pc), that one is at prorm.com. My biggest sell for a [Pi Hole](https://pi-hole.net/) is that it can be configured as a network wide ad-blocker, essentially filtering out known advertising DNS resolutions.
##### CopyParty PC:
I needed something to essentially function as a NAS but on a laptop that I acquired for extremely cheap right after the Windows 10 EOL and right before the great RAM / storage  shortage. I was considering learning TrueNAS for this but as it had only 500g of storage (less than the [Parent Pod](/articles/ipod#parent-pod---512g)...) and only on one ssd, so it really felt like overkill and I could not even use any of the redundancy features TrueNAS could offer. This is mostly for transferring files so redundancy is not all to important anyways. I chose to use [CopyParty](https://github.com/9001/copyparty), which is a single python file that does everything for me. One cronjob to run that python file every reboot and a static IP is all I needed. CopyParty can also play music files and mp4s directly from the browser, so its a great quick way to get a NAS and Jellyfin experience in one python script. Good enough, at least in my case, to make you want to set up dedicated NAS and Jellyfin containers in your [kubernetes cluster](/articles/kube).
##### tplink 5 port switch:

I ended up using the extra ports for an old cisco router in a DMZ anyways, let me play around with some old equipment without messing with my roommates network.
